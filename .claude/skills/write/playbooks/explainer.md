# Playbook — Explainer

For explaining how something works, what a concept means, or why something happens.
The viewer should **understand one idea clearly and feel smart for watching**.

## Defaults

| | |
|-|-|
| Length | 45–60 s (short), 2–4 min (long) — default 60 s |
| Hook window | first 3 s (≤ 8 spoken words) |
| Pace | 2.4 wps — clear, room to absorb |
| Tone | curious, friendly, confident; a smart friend, not a lecturer |
| Point of view | "you" + "we" when walking through steps |

## Hook formulas

1. **Hidden process** — reveal that something everyday has a surprising mechanism.
   "Every time you tap pay, four companies talk to each other."
2. **Curiosity question** — a question the viewer realises they can't answer.
   "Why do planes fly at thirty-five thousand feet, and not higher?"
3. **Myth bust** — a common belief, then "that's wrong".
   "Your phone isn't listening to you. It doesn't need to."
4. **Surprising fact** — a verified counter-intuitive fact.
   "There's more gold in a ton of phones than a ton of gold ore."
5. **Stakes** — why not knowing this costs the viewer.
   "If you don't understand this one number, your loan costs double."
6. **Simple-analogy tease** — promise a simple picture of a complex idea.
   "Blockchain in one sentence, with a notebook."

Facts in hooks must be verified and listed in Sources.

## Structure

Use these functions to check coverage after drafting continuous narration. They
are not separate sections to announce in VO; merge or reorder them to follow the
actual mechanism. Percentages are rough guides, not time boxes to fill.

| Beat | Share of time | Job |
|------|---------------|-----|
| Hook | ~8% | Open the question/loop |
| Context | ~15% | Why it matters / where the viewer meets this |
| Core explanation | ~45% | The mechanism, in 2–4 ordered steps, with one analogy |
| Example | ~15% | One concrete real-world case that makes it click |
| Takeaway | ~10% | One-sentence summary the viewer could repeat |
| CTA | ~7% | Follow / watch next / comment |

## Writing rules

- **One idea per video.** If there are two, it's two videos.
- **One analogy, used consistently.** Don't switch metaphors mid-video.
- **Preserve the process order and prerequisites.** Narrate the actions directly;
  use "first", "then" or "finally" only when they help understanding. Put useful
  step labels on screen rather than announcing a heading before explaining it.
- **Product or feature walkthroughs follow one thing through its life** (one test, one
  order, one lead), not the app's tabs in menu order. Bridge each feature to the next
  through the viewer's next need. Keep alternative creation methods before review
  and publishing, not after. Never "Step 1: <tab name>".
- **Define every term the first time**, in plain words, or don't use it.
- **Concrete before abstract.** Show the example, then name the concept.
- **Pay off the hook explicitly** — answer the exact question asked.
- ON-SCREEN text: labels for parts of a diagram, step numbers, the key term, the takeaway.

## CTA patterns

- "Follow for one 'how it works' every week."
- "Next, what happens when the payment fails. Part two!"
- "Comment what you want explained next."

## Avoid

- Jargon stacking ("the API calls the PSP via the NPCI switch")
- History lessons before the viewer cares
- Over-hedging ("sort of", "basically", "kind of")
- Unverified numbers — use `[UNVERIFIED]` and tell the user
