# Playbook — Promo / ad

For selling a product, service, event, app or offer. The viewer should **want it and
know exactly what to do next**.

## Defaults

| | |
|-|-|
| Length | 15 s (ad), 30 s (standard), 60 s (product story) — default 30 s |
| Hook window | first 2 s (≤ 6 spoken words) |
| Pace | 2.7 wps — energetic, no dead air |
| Tone | confident, warm, benefit-led; never desperate or hype-y |
| Point of view | "you" — speak to one viewer |

## Hook formulas

1. **Pain call-out** — name the exact frustration the audience feels.
   "Still spending Sunday doing your accounts?"
2. **Bold promise** — the result, with a specific number or timeframe.
   "Your website, live in 48 hours."
3. **Before / after** — show the transformation in one line.
   "This was a three-star café. Six weeks later… fully booked!"
4. **Pattern interrupt** — an unexpected statement that breaks the scroll.
   "Don't buy this. Unless you hate waiting in queues."
5. **Social proof first** — lead with the strongest real proof.
   "Ten thousand bakers switched last year. Here's why."
6. **Direct question to the target** — filters the audience instantly.
   "Own a clinic in Lucknow? Listen up."

Proof, numbers and offers in hooks must come from the brief. No invented reviews,
customer counts or results.

## Structure

Choose one central promise and relevant proof before the hook. Draft the narration
as a whole, then use these functions to check coverage. Merge, omit or reorder
functions when the angle warrants it (e.g. proof-led opening); percentages are
rough guides. Do not turn the functions into spoken headings or a company checklist.

| Beat | Share of time | Job |
|------|---------------|-----|
| Hook | ~10% | Stop the scroll, signal who this is for |
| Problem | ~15% | Make the pain specific and felt |
| Solution | ~25% | Introduce the product as the answer — name it clearly |
| Benefits / demo | ~25% | 2–3 benefits max, each as an outcome for the viewer, shown not told |
| Proof | ~10% | Testimonial, number, logo, award — only real ones |
| Offer + CTA | ~15% | The offer, urgency if real, one clear action |

For 15 s ads compress to: Hook → Solution + 1 benefit → CTA.

## Writing rules

- **Benefits over features.** "Charges in 20 minutes" → "Plug in during coffee, leave
  with a full battery."
- **Name the brand early** (by ~30% of runtime) and again in the CTA.
- **One CTA only.** One action, said once in VO and shown on screen with the exact
  link/handle/phone number.
- **Specific beats generic.** Select relevant supported details from the brief,
  not every available number, faculty name or service. Tie proof to the central
  promise and attribute individual customer results; never imply a guaranteed result.
- **Urgency only if true** (real deadline, real limited stock).
- ON-SCREEN text: price, offer, key benefit words, logo/CTA card at the end.

## CTA patterns

- "Book your free trial. The link is in the bio!"
- "Order today at <site>. Delivery tomorrow."
- "DM us 'START' to get your audit."

## Avoid

- Superlatives you can't prove ("best in India", "number one")
- More than 3 benefits
- Talking about the company before the viewer's problem
- Ending without the brand name and CTA on screen
- Claims regulated in the product's field (health, finance) unless the brief supplies
  approved wording
