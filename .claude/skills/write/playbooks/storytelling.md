# Playbook — Storytelling / documentary

For true stories, history, biographies, events, brand origin stories, mysteries.
The viewer should **feel something and need to know how it ends**.

## Defaults

| | |
|-|-|
| Length | 60–90 s (short), 3–8 min (long) — default 75 s |
| Hook window | first 3 s (≤ 10 spoken words) |
| Pace | 2.3 wps, varied — slow for weight, faster for action |
| Tone | cinematic, immersive, respectful; present tense for scenes |
| Point of view | narrator; "you" only to put the viewer in a scene |

## Hook formulas

1. **In medias res** — open at the most dramatic moment, then rewind.
   "At 3:47 a.m., the night guard heard the vault door close. From the inside."
2. **Ending first** — state the shocking outcome, make the viewer need the how.
   "He sold the company for a dollar. Ten years later it was worth billions."
3. **Put the viewer there** — second-person scene-setting.
   "You're nineteen, in a city where you don't speak the language, with forty rupees."
4. **Unanswered question** — a mystery the story resolves.
   "Nobody knows who built it. But we know why."
5. **Contrast** — two facts that shouldn't both be true.
   "The richest man in the world. Died with nothing."
6. **Quote** — a real, sourced line that carries tension.

## Structure

Draft the continuous story before dividing it. These are narrative functions, not
mandatory titled sections; combine or reorder them for the chosen story. Keep a
clear causal progression and distinguish an intentional flashback from a continuity error.

| Beat | Share of time | Job |
|------|---------------|-----|
| Hook | ~8% | Drop into tension |
| World / character | ~15% | Who, where, when, what they want |
| Inciting incident | ~12% | The thing that changes everything |
| Rising tension | ~30% | Obstacles, stakes rise, 2–3 escalating moments |
| Climax / turn | ~15% | The decisive moment — pays off the hook |
| Resolution + meaning | ~12% | What happened after, why it matters today |
| CTA | ~8% | Soft — follow for part 2 / more stories |

For a series, end on a cliffhanger instead of a full resolution and say so in story.md.

## Writing rules

- **Scenes, not summaries.** Specific time, place, sensory detail.
- **Short sentences at high tension.** Longer ones for reflection.
- **Use silence.** `VO: [SILENCE 1s]` after a big reveal; note it in DELIVERY.
- **One protagonist** the viewer follows; keep side characters to a minimum.
- **Accuracy matters most here.** Verify names, dates, places, quotes. Mark
  dramatised reconstructions ("what he might have said") clearly in VO, and never
  present invented dialogue as a real quote.
- **Sensitive subjects** (death, tragedy, crime victims): no sensationalism, no gore,
  name real victims only when publicly documented and relevant.
- ON-SCREEN text: date/place stamps ("Mumbai, 1993"), names with roles, key numbers.

## CTA patterns

- "Part two is out tomorrow, so follow and don't miss it!"
- "More forgotten stories on this channel."
- (Often best as on-screen only, letting the last VO line land.)

## Avoid

- Starting with background ("born in 1902 in…")
- Moralising at the end — one line of meaning is enough
- Unsourced quotes or dates
- More than one twist in a short
